Standing water in your home or garage right now

Emergency water removal is pumping and extracting standing water with submersible pumps and extraction units before it soaks walls and floors. Our 24/7 emergency line covers Cathedral City, and we send crews same-day when available, including nights and weekends. Every hour water sits, more of it climbs into drywall and cabinet bases.

While you wait, shut the main water valve if a pipe is the source, lift valuables and electronics off the floor, and keep people and pets out of dirty water. Shut off the breakers serving wet rooms only when you can reach the panel without crossing wet floor.

Safety around water, outlets and panels

If water is touching outlets, appliances or your electrical panel, do not wade in. Call Southern California Edison or 911 first, then call us. If you smell gas, leave the house and call The Gas Company or 911 before anything else.

How the water actually comes out

Deep water goes first, with a submersible pump dropped at the lowest point and discharged away from the house. Once the water is down to an inch or less, extraction units take over, either portable units carried inside or a truck-mounted unit with hose runs through a door. Wands then pull water from carpet and pad.

Pumping leaves the building soaked, not dry. After extraction, the crew checks the walls and floors with moisture meters, and drying begins as laid out on our water mitigation page.

Discharge matters more than people expect. Water pumped from a garage onto a neighbor's driveway or into a low spot beside the slab can find its way back. The crew picks a discharge point sloping away from your foundation, runs hose along it, and checks the flow once the pump is running.

Depth is only half the story. How long the water sat and what it picked up matter as much as the gallons.

Burst Pipe Water Damage

A burst pipe can dump gallons a minute, so the main shut-off comes first. It is usually at the front of the house near the meter or where the supply line enters the garage or a utility closet. Turn it clockwise until it stops, then open a lower faucet to drain what is left.

Next, look at the common failure points. Supply lines under sinks and behind toilets crack at the fittings, water heaters fail at the tank or the relief valve, and washing machine hoses split when they are old. Each has its own shut-off valve, and closing it can limit the damage while you wait.

Before the crew arrives, move rugs and boxes off wet floors and pull out drawers if water has reached cabinets. Take photos of the pipe, the wet area and the shut-off. Do not open ceilings that are bulging with water, because a controlled opening by the crew is safer.

Crawl spaces and whole-house flooding

If your home has a crawl space, basement or lower level, water there is slow to dry and hard to see. Whole-house flooding calls for several pumps running at once, a truck-mounted extractor and a plan for where the discharge goes. Large losses of this kind are covered under flood damage restoration.

Slab homes behave differently. Water spreads across the whole floor plate, creeps under walls into adjoining rooms and soaks the bottom edge of drywall and cabinet kicks. Pulling baseboards early lets extraction reach the wall base and lets air start moving through the cavity.

After the water is out

Wet carpet pad, saturated drywall and anything touched by dirty water are removed as part of water damage cleanup. Then the structure is dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Skipping that step is how a pumped-out room turns into a musty one a week later.

Keep your own notes during this stage. Write down when you first saw water, when you shut the valve, and who you called, since those details support the claim conversation with your insurer later.

What drives the cost of removal

  • Access, such as stairs, long hose runs and distance to discharge
  • Materials affected and drying days
  • Rebuild scope afterward

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle water removal emergencies at night or on weekends in Cathedral City?

Yes. Pump-out calls are taken at 2 a.m. the same as at noon. Same-day response is offered when crews are free, with no fixed arrival time, and the dispatcher coaches you through protecting the house meanwhile.

How do you remove water from carpet?

A wand extractor pulls water from the carpet and pad. If the pad is soaked or the water was dirty, it is lifted and removed, and the carpet is dried or replaced.

Can I use a wet vac myself?

For a small spill, yes, as long as nothing electrical is in the water. For anything deeper than a puddle, a shop vac cannot keep up and the water soaks into walls and subfloor.

What should I tell dispatch about my street?

Give your address, cross streets, the nearest alternate route and if your street has standing water. Roads in the city closed on October 11, 2026, and the crew needs to know how to reach you.

Will pumping alone fix the problem?

No. Pumping removes the standing water, but walls, flooring and subfloor stay wet. They need moisture readings and drying with air movers and dehumidifiers to avoid odors and further damage.
