Emergency steps

If water is spreading right now

The first hour decides how much of the building stays dry. These four steps are safe for most homes while you wait for a crew, and the dispatcher can talk you through them on the call.

1

Stop the source

Close the main water valve if the water comes from plumbing. If you are unsure where the valve is, ask the dispatcher to walk you through it.

2

Think power first

Stay out of water that touches outlets, appliances or the panel. Cut power at the panel only if the panel is dry and safe to reach.

3

Lift what you can

Move small valuables, papers and electronics off wet floors. Leave heavy furniture alone until a crew has looked at it.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Tell dispatch where the water is, how far it has spread and whether it looks clean, discolored or like sewage.

Insurance claims

Documentation your adjuster can use

Clear paperwork for any claim

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered by a homeowners policy. Gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

The California Department of Insurance advises homeowners to report damage promptly, keep a log of calls and correspondence, make temporary repairs to prevent further damage and be wary of early offers that require releasing further claims. Our crews take photos, moisture readings and a written scope so your adjuster has clear documentation. For large losses, that paperwork matters even more.

Local conditions

Water damage in Cathedral City: what the record shows

Cathedral City gets only about 4.85 inches of rain in an average year, mostly in winter, so a storm lands on dry ground and runs fast. The remnants of Rachel crossed the valley on October 11, 2026, and most of it got more than an inch of rain. Public Works crews set out barricades overnight, Ramon Road collected standing water and one westbound stretch of Dinah Shore Drive was closed.

If you call during a storm, give dispatch your cross streets, because the best route to you may not be the usual one.

The valley has seen worse before. During the Valentine's Day flood of 2019, the Cathedral Canyon low-water crossing lost its two northbound lanes and Cathedral Canyon Drive stayed closed for more than a month. A flood in August 1946 changed the rock formations of Cathedral Canyon itself, the feature that gave the city its name.

The lesson for a homeowner is simple: check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about flood coverage, because standard policies usually exclude flood.

Summer is the other pressure point. The mean July high is about 107.6 F, so air conditioners run hard for months and condensate lines and drain pans become a common leak source above ceilings. Repairs after any loss may need a permit, so ask the Building and Safety Division at 68700 Avenida Lalo Guerrero before you rebuild.

Mold after water damage

Water that stays in a wall, ceiling or closet for more than a couple of days can feed mold. Water damage questions answered

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Cathedral City?

Yes. The emergency line is open 24 hours a day, every day of the year, and calls are answered at night, on weekends and on holidays. We send crews with same-day response when available, but we do not promise a fixed arrival time, because road closures after a storm and the size of the loss both change how fast a crew can reach you.

While you wait, shut off the water at the main valve if the source is plumbing, keep people and pets out of the wet area and lift small valuables off the floor.

What does water damage restoration include?

It starts with stopping the source and removing standing water with pumps and extraction units. Next comes moisture mapping with meters and thermal imaging, then drying with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers until the readings match dry reference material.

Cleanup of affected materials and antimicrobial treatment follow when the water was contaminated. Rebuilding covers drywall, insulation, flooring and paint. Scope and price are explained before work starts, so you know which of these steps your loss needs.

How long does drying take?

It depends on the amount of water, the materials that got wet and the readings the crew takes each day. Wall cavities, insulation and subfloors dry far more slowly than a surface that only looks wet, even in a dry desert climate.

A crew checks moisture levels on each visit and removes the equipment only when the readings show the structure is dry. That log is also useful paperwork for your insurer.

Is water damage restoration the same as waterproofing?

No. Restoration deals with water that has already entered the building. Waterproofing and foundation work aim to keep water out in the future, and that is a different trade. If a restoration crew finds that water keeps coming in from the same spot, we will tell you so you can bring in a contractor for the cause.

Can I rent air movers and dehumidifiers and dry the house myself?

You can rent equipment, but placement and monitoring are what make drying work. Air movers aimed at the wrong surface can push moisture into wall cavities, and without daily readings you cannot tell when a wall is truly dry.

Our crews bring the drying equipment as part of the job and we do not list equipment rental as a service.

Will you work with my insurance adjuster?

We document the loss with photos, moisture readings, drying logs and a written scope so your adjuster has clear paperwork. We cannot promise how a claim will be decided, and we do not bill insurers directly. Read your policy or ask your insurer what it covers, and report the damage promptly.
