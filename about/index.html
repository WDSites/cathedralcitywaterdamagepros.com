About Cathedral City Water Damage Pros

A 24/7 emergency line for water damage and mold in Cathedral City and the nearby Coachella Valley towns.

Call (760) 769-1159

What we do

Cathedral City Water Damage Pros is a 24-hour dispatch line for water damage restoration and mold remediation. When you call, we ask what happened, where the water is coming from and whether anyone is in danger, then send licensed and insured restoration professionals to your address.

The work is organized into six services: emergency water removal, water mitigation, water damage cleanup, flood damage restoration, water damage repair and mold remediation. The services page explains which one fits your situation.

The standards we point to

The published industry standards for this trade are ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage restoration and ANSI/IICRC S520 for mold remediation. We name them because they are what an informed customer should ask about. When you hire any restoration company, ask whether its crews work to these standards and ask to see the drying log and moisture readings.

We do not publish a license number or certification claim on this site, and we do not display ratings. What we can promise is a clear scope and price before work starts and honest answers about what your loss needs.

What we do not do

  • We do not repair fire or smoke damage.
  • We do not do waterproofing or foundation work. If water keeps entering from the same place, a different contractor handles the cause.
  • We do not diagnose illness. Questions about how mold affects health belong to a physician.
  • We do not decide insurance claims or promise their outcome.

Who we serve

We take calls from homeowners, renters, landlords and small businesses. A renter can call about a leak in an apartment, although the landlord or property manager may need to approve the work and should be told about the damage right away. A business owner can call about a flooded storefront or office where staying open depends on how quickly the water is removed.

Large losses get the same first steps as small ones: stop the source, protect people from electrical and contamination risks, and document what is wet before anything is moved. The difference is the paperwork, because a bigger claim usually needs a longer drying log and a more detailed written scope.

Our home base is ZIP 92234 in Cathedral City. We also work in Palm Springs, Rancho Mirage, Desert Hot Springs, Palm Desert and Thousand Palms, and each of those towns has its own page on the service areas page with the ZIP codes and local conditions that matter for water damage.

How a call works

The dispatcher first asks whether anyone is in danger and whether water is touching electrical equipment. If it is, you are told to stay out and to call the electric utility or 911 before anything else. Then the dispatcher asks where the water is coming from, how far it has spread, whether it looks clean, discolored or like sewage, and which rooms are wet.

From those answers the dispatcher can tell you what to do while you wait, such as closing the main valve or lifting valuables off the floor, and can send the crew that fits the job. Scope and price are explained before work starts. We do not promise an arrival time, because storm closures and the size of the loss both change the route and the schedule.

Questions to ask any restoration company

These questions are worth asking of us and of any other company you call. A company that answers them plainly is usually one that documents its work.

  • Do your crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500 for water or S520 for mold?
  • Will you keep a drying log with daily moisture readings?
  • Will the scope and price be explained before work starts?
  • Which equipment will be placed, and when will it be removed?
  • What documentation will I get for my insurer?
  • What parts of this job will a different contractor need to handle?

Our answers are on the service pages, for example the drying log and equipment details under water mitigation and the containment and clearance testing steps under mold remediation.
