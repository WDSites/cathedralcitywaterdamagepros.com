What Flood Damage Restoration Means

Flood damage restoration is the removal of storm, wash or groundwater from a building, followed by cleaning, disinfecting, drying and rebuilding what the water ruined. In Cathedral City, a flooded home is an emergency, and our 24/7 emergency line takes these calls at any hour. Same-day response is available when crews are free, and the first few hours decide how much flooring, drywall and furniture can be saved.

Floodwater is different from a broken pipe. It picks up soil, fuel, animal waste and sewage on its way in, so it is classed as category 2 or 3 water. That changes which materials can be dried and which must be thrown out.

Floodwater Is Inside Your Home Right Now

Get everyone out of standing water first. Stay out of any water that touches outlets, cords, appliances or the electrical panel. Leave the panel alone if reaching it means wading, and ask the electric utility to cut service instead. For any electrical or gas risk, call 911, Southern California Edison or The Gas Company before you call us.

While you wait for a crew, you can do a few safe things:

  • Stop the source if you can, such as closing the main water valve for a supply failure
  • Lift valuables, papers, electronics and small furniture onto tables or counters
  • Keep children and pets out of the water, and do not wade through it barefoot
  • Take photos and video of the water line and damaged items before you move anything
  • Do not run a household vacuum on water, and do not drive through flooded roads

Call the 24/7 line once the immediate safety steps are done. Nights and weekends are handled the same as weekdays.

Why Desert Storms Flood Fast

On October 11, 2026, the remnants of Tropical Storm Rachel dropped more than an inch of rain across most of the Coachella Valley. Standing water closed Dinah Shore Drive westbound between Date Palm Drive and Cathedral Canyon Drive, and Vista Chino at the wash. Fire Station 2 handed out sandbags. If your street was one of those, check garage thresholds and flooring now.

The city averages only about 4.85 inches of rain a year, mostly in winter. That is reasoning, not a claim about soil: ground that rarely gets soaked can shed a sudden storm quickly instead of absorbing it, so runoff reaches driveways and garage doors fast. Know where your lot drains before the next forecast, and keep sandbags where you can reach them.

The Valentine's Day flood of 2019 closed Cathedral Canyon Drive for more than a month, and the low-water crossing lost its two northbound lanes to flow through the wash channel. When a crossing fails, your route can fail with it. Tell the dispatcher your cross streets and the route you can really drive.

The August 1946 flood even reshaped the rock formations of Cathedral Canyon, which gives the city its name. Washes that carried water then can carry it again, so look up your address on the FEMA flood map.

Lower-Level, Garage and Crawl Space Flooding Cleanup

Water runs downhill and settles in the lowest rooms. In a typical desert home that means the garage, a sunken living area, a crawl space if your home has one, or a basement or lower level if yours does. Each holds water longer than you expect, because it has nowhere to drain.

Crews start with pumps and extraction units, working from the deepest point outward. A trash pump moves heavy mud and silt, and a truck-mounted or portable extractor lifts what is left from carpet and slab. Mud is shoveled out before drying begins, because drying over silt just bakes it into the floor.

In a crawl space, wet insulation and vapor barrier are pulled, joists are checked with a moisture meter, and air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings settle. Drywall that wicked floodwater is cut off at a flood-cut height above the highest wet line.

Sewage Backup Cleanup

A sewage backup is category 3 water and an emergency from the first minute. Keep everyone out of the room, wear gloves and boots only if you must enter, and never use fixtures that feed the line. Stay out of water that touches electrical outlets, and call 911 or the utility for any power or gas risk before you call us.

Crews contain the area with plastic sheeting, wear protective gear, extract the liquid, and remove porous materials that touched it, including carpet, pad and low drywall. Hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected, then the structure is dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Porous materials are not salvaged after sewage contact.

Some Cathedral City neighborhoods, including Whispering Palms, South City, and Papaya and Pomegranate, are still on septic systems, and the city has asked residents for feedback on a possible septic-to-sewer project.

Know if your home is on septic or sewer before a backup happens. A septic backup can point to a full tank or drain field and needs a septic service as well. A sewer backup may involve the main line. The dispatcher will want to know which one you have.

How the Restoration Is Done

The sequence is the same on every flood job, scaled to size. Extraction comes first, then removal of materials that cannot be saved, then cleaning and disinfecting, then structural drying and a final moisture check. Scope and price are explained before work starts, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500.

If the flood loss also involves standing water in several rooms, emergency water removal is the first phase, followed by water damage cleanup for debris and sanitizing. Wet drywall, framing and carpet that stay damp past the first few days can grow mold, which is covered under mold remediation.

Flood Insurance and Your Policy

Flood damage is usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy and needs separate flood coverage. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer what your policy covers before you need it. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, but rising water from outside usually is not.

California's residential claims guide advises you to report damage to your agent or company, keep a log of calls and correspondence, make temporary repairs to prevent further damage, and be wary of early offers that ask you to release further liability. Photos and our drying logs help document the loss.

What Affects the Cost of a Flood Job

  • Affected area and how deep the water got
  • Materials, such as carpet, hardwood, drywall height and insulation
  • Drying days and how many air movers and dehumidifiers run
  • Rebuild scope after demolition

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle flood damage restoration emergencies at night or on weekends in Cathedral City?

Yes. Our 24/7 emergency line takes flood calls at night, on weekends and on holidays, and same-day response is available when crews are free. Arrival times are not promised, because closed roads can change the route, but you will hear what the first visit involves.

Is storm floodwater safe to clean up myself?

Treat it as contaminated. It can carry soil, fuel, animal waste and sewage, and it can hide electrical hazards. Stay out of water that touches outlets or the panel, and call a crew with pumps and protective gear for anything beyond a small puddle.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?

Usually not. Flood is generally excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy and the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask your insurer what applies to your situation.

How do I know if I am on septic or sewer?

Look at your closing papers, property records or utility bills, and check your yard for a tank lid. The city has asked residents in Whispering Palms, South City, and Papaya and Pomegranate for feedback on a possible septic-to-sewer project, so those neighborhoods are worth confirming.

What should I do before the crew arrives?

Get people and pets out of the water, handle electrical and gas risks first, lift valuables, and photograph the damage. Do not drive through flooded roads. Turn around, don't drown.
