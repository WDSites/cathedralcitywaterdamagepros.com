A year of water in Palm Desert

Palm Desert sits at about 220 feet of elevation, with a 2020 population of 51,163, and its water trouble follows the calendar more than the weather report. Rain is light and mostly lands in winter. Cathedral City next door averages about 4.85 inches a year. So most claims here start with a pipe, a drain line or a sprinkler, and a few start with a storm.

This page walks through the year the way a restoration crew sees it. Each season has its own usual suspect, and each one calls for a different first move. We lead with water mitigation because stopping the spread in the first day decides almost everything that follows.

Winter: short rains on hard ground

When a winter front does arrive, desert ground sheds water instead of soaking it up. Runoff hunts for the lowest door threshold, the garage slab or the gap under a patio slider. Check weatherstripping and the grade beside your foundation before the forecast.

If water has already crossed a threshold, the job is extraction first, then drying. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull the standing water, and then air movers and an LGR dehumidifier work on the baseboards and the bottom of the drywall. Our emergency water removal crews start with the pumps.

Spring: the quiet months that hide slow leaks

Spring is dry and mild, which is when a weeping supply line under a vanity or a sweating water heater goes unnoticed. A cabinet floor that feels soft or a baseboard that has started to bubble is a moisture problem, not a paint problem.

A moisture meter and a thermal camera find the wet material behind the finish. That mapping tells us whether we are drying one wall or opening three. Keep those readings for your insurer.

Summer: when the AC never rests

Mean July highs in this part of the valley reach about 107 degrees, so cooling systems run for hours every day. A condensate drain line clogs, the drain pan overflows into the ceiling or closet, and the first sign is a brown stain. Have the line flushed before June, and ask your AC technician where the pan sits.

Attic and closet leaks soak insulation, which holds water like a sponge and keeps the drywall wet long after the dripping stops. That is a water damage cleanup job: wet insulation comes out, the framing dries, and the area is treated before anyone rebuilds.

Fall: the storm tail and October 11, 2026

On October 11, 2026, the remnants of Tropical Storm Rachel put well over two inches of rain on Palm Desert in a single event. Standing water turned the College of the Desert driving range into a pond-like field. If rain that heavy can saturate open turf, it can also push through a garage door or a low patio entry.

After a storm like that, walk your property once the water drops. Look at the garage threshold, the slab edges and any drain that is slow to clear. Mud and silt left inside a home count as flood-type debris, so treat the area as contaminated and call for flood damage restoration.

If water is still spreading in Palm Desert

Find the valve

Look for the main shut-off at the front of the house or beside the water heater. Turn it clockwise until it stops. A leaking toilet or sink has its own stop valve under the fixture.

Be careful with power

Standing water near outlets means stay out. Switch off the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Gas smell or sparking means 911 first.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, laptops, paperwork and small furniture onto counters or dry rooms.

Keep clear of dirty water

Water from a toilet, a drain or the outdoors carries bacteria. Keep children and pets out and wear boots if you must cross it.

Call and take photos

Photograph the damage before moving anything, then call our 24/7 emergency line. Tell your insurance agent as well and keep a log of your calls.

El Paseo and the commercial side of water loss

Shops along El Paseo, south of Highway 111, have glass storefronts, finished floors and inventory on low shelves. A sprinkler head failure or a restaurant dishwasher line can put water into a dozen tenant spaces at once. We stage drying so a business can reopen its front rooms while the back rooms are still being dried.

The same logic applies to larger venues such as the McCallum Theatre or a campus building at College of the Desert. Carpeted aisles and stage floors need drying logs and moisture readings before anything is closed up.

Golf course edges and irrigation lines

With about thirty golf courses in town, including Palm Desert Country Club and Desert Willow Golf Resort, many homes sit on a fairway, a pond edge or a shared irrigation main. A stuck valve can saturate a stem wall or soak a lawn side of the slab for days. Tell your HOA board if a patch stays soggy.

Wet exterior walls can wick moisture into stucco and the drywall behind it. When that happens, water damage repair follows the drying step and covers the drywall, insulation and paint.

Who to call before the meter

Coachella Valley Water District is the water agency for Palm Desert. If you hear water running or see a wet patch near the street side of the meter, call CVWD about the leak, since that side belongs to the utility. Anything after the meter, including the pipe in your wall, is yours and is where we come in.

Is emergency water removal available in Palm Desert?

Yes. Our emergency line is staffed 24/7 and we offer same-day response when available. We do not promise an arrival time, but we will tell you honestly what to expect when you call.

What should I do first if an AC drain line floods my ceiling in summer?

Turn the AC off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. Then catch what you can with buckets, move belongings, and call for extraction before the insulation soaks through.

Does storm water in a garage need professional drying?

If water reached drywall, carpet or stored boxes, yes. Mud and runoff are not clean water, so wet porous materials usually come out and the slab and framing are dried and treated.
